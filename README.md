## Critical Listening and Immersive Experience

My critical-listening development began with extensive structured [SoundGym training](https://www.soundgym.co/member/profile?m=byy1s26wj7u): 9,491 completed activities, a 3,410,130 SPI, and the Diamond Ears Award. Although SoundGym labels them “games,” they are auditory-training exercises that often contain more than one listening trial. I then used James Corey’s software-based listening tests for EQ, compression, and related technical-ear-training categories in class and have continued practicing with them independently. The current [Corey-software training record](https://github.com/kevinworleymusic-afk/technical-ear-training) contains at least 3,491 logged trials, including 87% across 1,000 +3 dB one-third-octave pink-noise trials and the first separately documented “Fast Car” music-transfer blocks, with task-specific limitations preserved alongside the results.

Additional experience includes instruction from [Ed Seay](https://aes.digitellinc.com/b/sp/ed-seay-3855), guided 7.1.4 Dolby Atmos listening, the [Ocean Way immersive study](Projects/Technical-Audio-and-Research/Ocean-Way-Jazz-Combo-Dolby-Atmos-Independent-Study/README.md), and listener—not author—participation in three published AES studies.

## How the Diagnostic Evidence Was Produced

The tuning results were generated through **prompt-based diagnostic exercises**, not by writing polished conclusions after the fact. Each scenario supplied bounded evidence such as frequency range, measured response behavior, crossover location, driver health, source combinations, listener or microphone movement, speaker geometry, electrical status, or vehicle conditions. The learner then gave an initial diagnosis in natural language before review or correction.

Later cases added a second pass in which the learner converted a correct diagnosis into a concise engineering sentence. The underlying tuning journal preserves representative **prompt/evidence → initial reasoning → review/refinement → engineering takeaway** sequences so a reader can see what information was supplied and what reasoning was independently produced.

Representative examples include a mild harness-loss decision, left/right electrical mismatch, time-varying midbass behavior, a ~400 Hz crossover-region dip, a 70–90 Hz passenger-seat cabin null, and the later engineering-sentence exercises.

## Progress Observed

By the later drills, the first-pass mechanism classification was becoming more reliable. Stronger areas included recognizing combined-source interaction, using crossover location and bandwidth as evidence, identifying high-frequency directivity/geometry problems, separating acoustic position dependence from hardware faults, and avoiding EQ before the physical mechanism was understood.

Remaining refinement areas were increasingly narrow: retrieving terms such as **cabin mode/modal cancellation**, separating directivity from reflection/interference, keeping **LFE** distinct from generic bass/subwoofer behavior, distinguishing timing integration from magnitude-response cancellation, and making the final engineering wording concise.

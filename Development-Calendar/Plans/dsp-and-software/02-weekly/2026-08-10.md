## Technical Listening Trainer Development

Earlier in the week, Kevin substantially developed the Python-based **Technical Listening Trainer** as a tool for his own structured critical-listening practice. The working prototype grew beyond simple A/B playback into a configurable EQ-training environment with blind/randomized A/B presentation, separate DSP processing, selectable positive and negative EQ gain, octave and 1/3-octave frequency choices, and continuing work on adjustable Q and more advanced trial behavior.

The trainer is being developed as an audio-training tool first, while also providing hands-on practice with Python, DSP implementation, debugging, Git/GitHub, GUI design, and software organization. AI-assisted development through ChatGPT and GitHub Copilot is documented separately rather than represented as unaided software-development work.

Current development is focused on improving the **automobile listening settings** so the trainer can provide useful automotive-oriented listening conditions while retaining controlled comparisons. The goal is to make these settings reliable enough to become part of regular ear-training work rather than simply adding simulation features for their own sake.

## Critical-Listening Progress

Current James Corey frequency-training work has reached the practical maximum difficulty available in the present exercise configuration at **3 dB**:

- Pink-noise peak/boost identification: **three simultaneous +3 dB boosts**, now identified at **nearly 100% accuracy reliably**.
- Pink-noise cut identification: **three simultaneous −3 dB cuts**, currently identified at approximately **90% accuracy**.

These results represent the current ceiling of the Corey frequency exercise being used: the task is already at 3 dB with three simultaneous EQ changes. Rather than continuing to make the pink-noise version artificially harder outside that exercise, the next step is to transfer the same listening skill to **existing music/song material**, where program-dependent spectral content and masking should create a more realistic challenge.

## Next Development / Training Steps

- Begin multi-band +3 dB / −3 dB frequency-identification practice on existing songs rather than pink noise alone.
- Continue refining and validating the Technical Listening Trainer's automobile settings for practical automotive-focused ear training.
- Continue integrating adjustable Q, multiple EQ changes, and other controls where they improve the training task.
- Longer term, add an **adaptive-difficulty feature inspired by the Harman listener-training software**, in which difficulty changes in response to demonstrated accuracy and user ability during the test. This is a planned feature, not a completed capability.

## Evidence and Claims Boundary

The listening percentages above are personal training results, not controlled research results or standardized professional qualification scores. The Harman-style adaptive system is a future design goal inspired by the concept of ability-responsive listener training; it is not claimed as a reproduction of proprietary Harman software. The Technical Listening Trainer remains an evolving personal training/development tool.

- **Status:** In progress.

## Listening Progress

Frequency-identification work in the James Corey training software has reached the current exercise ceiling at **3 dB**. On pink noise, Kevin is now reliably identifying **three simultaneous +3 dB boosts at nearly 100% accuracy** and **three simultaneous −3 dB cuts at approximately 90% accuracy**.

The next progression is to move the same multi-band boost/cut task onto **existing songs**, where spectral density, instrumentation, and masking should make identification more representative of real program-material listening than pink noise alone.

## Technical Listening Trainer

Earlier in the week, Kevin substantially developed his Python-based **Technical Listening Trainer** for self-directed critical-listening practice. The trainer provides a platform for controlled A/B listening and configurable EQ exercises and is being expanded toward automotive-oriented training use cases.

Current work is centered on **refining the automobile settings** so they produce useful, repeatable training conditions. The purpose is to support ear-training practice under more vehicle-relevant listening conditions while keeping the test variable controlled enough for meaningful comparison.

A longer-term software goal is to add an **adaptive-difficulty system similar in concept to the Harman listener-training approach**, allowing the trainer to increase or decrease difficulty based on accuracy and demonstrated user ability during a session.

## Next Reasonable Scope

- Transfer current 3 dB, three-band frequency-identification performance from pink noise to existing songs.
- Tune and validate the trainer's automobile settings before relying on them for regular training.
- Use listening results to determine which conditions should become easier/harder rather than increasing complexity arbitrarily.
- Develop adaptive difficulty only after the underlying trial generation, scoring, and condition handling are stable.

## Evidence and Claims Boundary

The accuracy figures are personal training results and are not presented as standardized professional qualification scores. The adaptive-difficulty feature is planned and is described as conceptually similar to ability-responsive Harman listener training, not as a reproduction of proprietary Harman software.
